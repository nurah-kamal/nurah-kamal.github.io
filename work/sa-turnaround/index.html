The question

Turnaround reports usually subtract one date from another and call it done. But a service desk’s targets are written in working days: two, three or five of them. Checked in calendar days, every weekend and public holiday inside a request counts against the desk.

So: how much does counting the wrong days change the picture, and once that’s out of the way, which delays are real?

What I found

Counting calendar days marked 1,183 requests late. 283 of those were on time once public holidays were counted, and 160 real delays slipped through.

With holidays out of the way, one real problem was left: refunds, which went from 19% late to 56% after a second sign-off step was added in July 2025. No other category moved.

Complaints did jump to 34% late for one quarter, then fell back to 16%. With about 40 complaints a quarter that is noise, and the write-up says so rather than calling it a second finding.

How I checked it

  • Measured in the unit the target is written in. A “3 working day” target is checked in working days.
  • Every number on the page comes from SQL. Each chart names the file that produced it, under the chart. Nothing is typed in by hand.
  • Two independent answers are made to agree. SQL works out working days from a calendar table; a JavaScript library works them out day by day. For all 5,706 closed requests the two are compared, and the tests fail if they ever disagree.
  • Planted, then found. The data generator planted one change, the refund sign-off. The tests check that the analysis still finds it and that no other category moved. The holiday effect wasn’t planted: it comes from the calendar alone.
  • A holiday is sourced, not remembered. The library follows the Public Holidays Act, including the Sunday rule, and every once-off declared day is listed with the page it was checked against.
  • No person is in the data. Not a real one and not a made-up one.

The principle behind it

Check the source before trusting the number.

Often the interesting part isn’t the analysis; it’s finding out whether the data is telling the truth in the first place. Here the late rate looked like a performance problem, and most of it was a counting problem.

The idea comes from reporting I do professionally, where turnaround has to account for South African public holidays. This version was rebuilt from scratch on invented data, and no work code or data is in it.

Worth knowing

  • The data is synthetic. It shows the method, not any real organisation’s performance.
  • National holidays only. Provincial days off and company shutdowns are out of scope.
  • A working day starts the day after opening: a request opened and closed on the same day took 0 working days.
